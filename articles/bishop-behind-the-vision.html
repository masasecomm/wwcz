Reverend Kathy Kiuna: The Bishop Behind the Vision

A look at Reverend Kathy Kiuna?s ministry, leadership and the vision behind her work.

By Warrior Woman Magazine editorial26 April 2026

The Bishop BEHIND the Vision

Before the world celebrated Reverend Kathy Kiuna as a woman without

limits, there stood beside her a man whose life was wholly consecrated to God—Bishop Allan Kiuna. His consecration into the Bishopric was not merely a ceremonial elevation; it was the recognition of a life already

poured out in service, sacrifice, and unwavering obedience to divine assignment.

Consecration speaks of separation unto the sacred—of a life set apart for holy purpose. In Bishop Allan’s journey, this was evident long before hands were laid upon him. His life reflected sanctification, discipline, and a steadfast devotion to God. He carried the weight of apostolic grace with humility, understanding that true authority in the Kingdom is born of surrender. From the humble beginnings in small restaurants in Nairobi to the establishment of a thriving ministry, his path was marked by audacious faith. He often declared that if he had waited for money to obey God, the vision would never have begun. Like the Genesis pattern—“And God said… and God saw”—he spoke what he believed long before it materialized. What others called impossibility, he called potential. What others feared, he confronted with faith.

The journey was not without cost. Auctioned property, financial hardship, uncertainty, and seasons of lack tested their resolve. Yet armed only with a promise and conviction, he pressed forward. He believed that hardship was never designed to destroy, but to develop—to convert pain into power and trials into testimony. >

To many, Bishop Allan Kiuna was more than a preacher; he was a father. Like David in the cave of Adullam, he drew to himself men and women in distress, debt, and discontent—and through mentorship, teaching, and example, transformed them into leaders. He had an uncommon grace to see greatness in people before they saw it in themselves. Young men and women across nations stand today as living proof of his apostleship.

He was a visionary and a strategist—restless in the face of mediocrity, allergic to stagnation. Excellence was not optional to him; it was a reflection of his reverence for God. He dreamed expansively—a state-of-the-art sanctuary, holistic spaces that served families, environments where faith and life converged seamlessly. He saw tomorrow with clarity and pursued it with disciplined focus.

His passion for education was equally profound. Quietly and generously, he invested in destinies—paying school fees, sponsoring studies, opening doors for those who lacked opportunity. Many beneficiaries of his kindness may never fully tell their stories, but heaven records them all.

Above all, he was a worshipper and a man of prayer. He was not casual with God. Scripture was not material for sermons; it was the substance of his life. Prayer, fasting, study—these were not events but rhythms. His authority flowed from intimacy. His preaching was an overflow of a hidden walk with God.

Yet perhaps one of his greatest legacies was the woman who stands today as Lady Bishop. He discerned the call of God upon her life and nurtured it intentionally. He called forth her voice, affirmed her mandate to women, and championed her leadership long before many understood it. In her own words, he drew out the gift within her and encouraged her to step boldly into her divine assignment. Together, they built more than a church; they built people. Together, they modeled covenant partnership in ministry. Together, they proved that vision, when rooted in God, can outlive opposition, scarcity, and even death. WWM
